# Milestone Next-Step Assessment (v1.45)

**Assessment Date:** 2026-05-28
**Target Milestone:** v1.45

## 1. Framing & Current State
Accrue is a fully-featured, idiomatically Elixir billing library for Phoenix SaaS developers. With the recent completion of v1.44 (Recovered-Revenue Dashboard), Accrue now proves its own ROI visually to adopters. The canonical SaaS loop (bill → change/cancel → recover → self-serve → gate access → operate) is completely shipped, including deep reporting on the recovery loop.

**Current State:** >95% "done" for its core stated scope. The foundation is robust, the documentation is comprehensive, the adopter story is complete, and we have a dashboard proving the dunning engine's value. We are firmly in an intake-gated maintenance mode, with very few high-leverage "polish" wedges remaining.

## 2. Adopter Coverage Map
- **Well-served:** Subscriptions, checkout, basic metering, webhook reconciliation, simple entitlements, email-based dunning, operator admin, dunning visibility (Recovered Revenue Dashboard), local developer environments (Fake lane).
- **Partially-served / Still-rough:** Complex multi-channel dunning notifications (e.g., in-app banners, SMS); rich metered/tiered entitlement math (which borders on overbuilding for our scope).

## 3. Candidate Research & Recommendations

We researched the top remaining deferred items to evaluate their leverage.

### Candidate A: Multi-channel Dunning (In-App Banners)
**Goal:** Expand dunning beyond email by providing an idiomatic Phoenix LiveView component (or guide) for displaying "Update your card" banners directly within the host application.
- **Pros/Cons:** In-app banners dramatically increase recovery rates because they catch the user while they are actively engaged with the software, avoiding spam folders entirely. It is low compliance risk compared to SMS. The con is that it requires tighter UI integration with the host application's layout.
- **Idiomatic Elixir:** Provide a headless `Accrue.Dunning.Banner` component or a `Accrue.Dunning.requires_attention?/1` helper that the host app can drop into their root `app.html.heex` or `on_mount` lifecycle.
- **Lessons Learned:** 
  - *Stripe:* In-app alerts are the most effective recovery tool.
  - *DX:* Do not dictate CSS. Provide a functionally complete but minimally styled component, or simply the state-checking helper.

### Candidate B: Multi-channel Dunning (SMS/Push via Chimeway)
**Goal:** Expand dunning to SMS or push notifications using the existing Chimeway adapter.
- **Pros/Cons:** High visibility, but introduces significant TCPA/GDPR compliance risks. Host apps must manage opt-in consent.
- **Idiomatic Elixir:** Treat Accrue as the scheduler, delegating the actual dispatch to the host app or the Chimeway adapter.
- **Lessons Learned:** Often better left as a "bring your own notifier" integration rather than a first-party feature to keep the library footprint small.

### Candidate C: Rich Metered/Tiered Entitlement Math
**Goal:** Enable volume discounts, graduated tiers, and complex usage math evaluated locally.
- **Pros/Cons:** Avoids Stripe latency for complex checks, but risks synchronization drift and adds massive complexity to the local Ecto schemas.
- **Idiomatic Elixir:** Would require ETS/GenServer batchers to avoid Postgres contention.
- **Lessons Learned:** Overbuilding. This crosses the line from a billing library to a full CPQ (Configure, Price, Quote) system.

---

## 4. The Perfect Recommendation: The Highest Leverage Next Step

**The Pick for v1.45:** **Candidate A — Multi-channel Dunning (In-App Banners)**

**Why:** It is the highest-leverage, lowest-risk next step. Email dunning is often ignored; in-app banners are impossible to miss and do not carry the compliance risks of SMS. By shipping an idiomatic helper or component, we complete the dunning story and help adopters save even more money, perfectly complementing the v1.44 dashboard. 

**What "Done Enough" Looks Like:**
- A simple API (e.g., `Accrue.Dunning.requires_attention?(customer_id)`) to check if a banner should be shown.
- An optional, minimally styled headless LiveComponent or functional component (`<Accrue.Components.DunningBanner />`).
- Documentation in `guides/dunning.md` showing how to mount it in a Phoenix app's root layout.

**Suggested Ordering After v1.45:**
1. **v1.46+:** Stop. Shift to pure maintenance mode (bug fixes, dependency bumps). Candidate B (SMS) and Candidate C (Tiered Math) are diminishing returns and outside the core mandate.

## 5. Blunt Maintainer Takeaway
*If I were you, what would I build next?*
Ship the In-App Dunning Banner helper/component, and then declare victory. Accrue is effectively feature-complete. Do not build SMS dunning or CPQ math. Provide the hooks for host apps to build what they need, but keep the core library lean and focused on the 80% use case. After v1.45, switch exclusively to a bug-fix and maintenance posture.
